Strategic Imperative

Why Machine Learning is a Board agenda item now

Peers are industrialising AI; regulators expect model risk discipline; clients expect personalised, real-time decisions.

Competitive pressure

Regional and global banks are embedding ML in credit decisioning, pricing, and CRM — widening the gap on cost-to-serve and share of wallet.

Regulatory expectation

Supervisors increasingly expect explainable models, robust model risk management, and demonstrable AML effectiveness — not spreadsheet controls.

Economic timing

Cloud platforms, data lakes, and talent markets now make enterprise ML economically viable at QNB’s scale — with 18–24 month payback on priority cases.

Client expectation

Corporate and retail clients expect instant onboarding, tailored offers, and frictionless fraud protection — experiences that only ML can sustain at volume.

Next-best-action engine

Unified scoring across channels recommends the right product, timing, and channel — lifting conversion while reducing irrelevant outreach.

Compliance & Financial Crime

Effectiveness up, noise down

Supervisors judge outcomes, not alert volume. ML shifts the compliance operating model from rule sprawl to risk-sensitive detection.

AML transaction monitoring redesign

Supervised and unsupervised models score alerts by true-risk likelihood, suppress low-value noise, and prioritise investigator queues.

2–3× investigator productivity
